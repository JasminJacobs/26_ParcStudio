Hundreds of thousands march across France in protests over schools and policing

Zoom-out to take a step back

Bias Comparison

  • More than 400,000 people joined protests across France over school funding, living costs and public services, according to the Interior Ministry.
  • The Paris march was mostly peaceful until masked protesters threw objects and fireworks at police, who used tear gas, sound grenades and water cannons.
  • Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said police made nearly 1,000 arrests, while 70,000 officers were deployed nationwide.
  • The protests grew from student demonstrations into a broader movement involving workers, unions and students.
  • On Saturday, October 24, nationwide protests drew between 410,000 and nearly 800,000 people across France, with 70,000 police deployed to counter potential violence according to the Interior Ministry and union organizers.
  • Sparked by high school staffing shortages last month, the movement evolved into a nationwide challenge as students and workers demanded increased education funding and relief from soaring living costs.
  • Police deployed water cannons and tear gas across multiple cities, while major Paris museums closed pre-emptively; government officials reported nearly 1,000 arrests as authorities attempted to prevent widespread looting.
  • Political pressure intensified on President Emmanuel Macron as far-left candidate Jean-Luc Melenchon and the far-right National Rally criticized his handling of the crisis; Melenchon warned that "this is going to repeat itself every week."
  • The Yellow Vests and labor unions announced further demonstrations for October 31 and November 5, demanding the freedom to protest and challenging the government's alleged "authoritarian drift" and fiscal policies.
  • Hundreds of thousands of people marched across France over high living costs, education funding, wages, and public services; the Interior Ministry reported 410,000 demonstrators nationwide, including 110,000 in Paris.
  • France deployed 70,000 police officers, while authorities changed the Paris march route and banned some rallies to reduce the risk of renewed violence.
  • Clashes occurred in Lyon and Paris, where police used tear gas; authorities reported 135 arrests nationwide.
  • Businesses, museums, and other cultural institutions closed or increased security after about 3,000 shops were damaged or looted the previous weekend.
