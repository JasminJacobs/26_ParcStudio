Hundreds of thousands march across France in protests over schools and policing

Bias
Comparison
  • On Saturday, October 24, nationwide protests drew between 410,000 and nearly 800,000 people across France, with 70,000 police deployed to counter potential violence according to the Interior Ministry and union organizers.
  • Sparked by high school staffing shortages last month, the movement evolved into a nationwide challenge as students and workers demanded increased education funding and relief from soaring living costs.
  • Police deployed water cannons and tear gas across multiple cities, while major Paris museums closed pre-emptively; government officials reported nearly 1,000 arrests as authorities attempted to prevent widespread looting.
  • Political pressure intensified on President Emmanuel Macron as far-left candidate Jean-Luc Melenchon and the far-right National Rally criticized his handling of the crisis; Melenchon warned that "this is going to repeat itself every week."
  • The Yellow Vests and labor unions announced further demonstrations for October 31 and November 5, demanding the freedom to protest and challenging the government's alleged "authoritarian drift" and fiscal policies.
